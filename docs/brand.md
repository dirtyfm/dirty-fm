Below is a usable brand identity direction for **DirtyFM / Drift** as a raw internet-radio/video/blog brand.

## Core brand idea

**DirtyFM is pirate-radio chaos for people who are sick of being told how to think.**

It should feel like:

> A late-night basement broadcast, a back-alley public access show, a prank call archive, a conspiracy corkboard, and a drunk free-speech rant all smashed into one dirty signal.

Not “clean rebel brand.”
Not “polished edgy podcast.”
Not “political movement.”
More like:

**unfiltered speech + dark comedy + anti-control energy + absurd human garbage fire.**

The site should feel like Drift grabbed the internet by the collar and yelled:

> “Say the thing. Don’t sanitize it. Don’t ask permission.”

---

# Brand positioning

## One-line positioning

**DirtyFM is raw, uncensored internet radio for loudmouths, degenerates, trolls, skeptics, and anyone who still believes people should be allowed to say wild shit.**

## Cleaner version for public-facing use

**DirtyFM is an unfiltered comedy and talk-radio archive built around free speech, chaos, prank culture, dark humor, and anti-control commentary.**

## More aggressive version

**DirtyFM is random fucking bullshit with a signal: prank calls, rants, dark comedy, anti-government noise, and open-mic insanity for people who hate being managed.**

## Brand promise

DirtyFM does not promise truth, taste, safety, or politeness.

It promises:

**you will hear something real, stupid, offensive, funny, uncomfortable, or impossible to predict.**

That unpredictability is the product.

---

# Main character: Drift

Drift should not be presented like a normal “host.”

He is the brand’s gravitational center.

## Drift archetype

**The outlaw broadcaster.**

Part pirate radio host.
Part barroom philosopher.
Part troll.
Part dirtbag comedian.
Part anti-government crank.
Part chaos conductor.

He does not need to be “right.”
He needs to be impossible to domesticate.

## Drift’s traits

Drift is:

* Loud-mouthed
* Anti-control
* Anti-government
* Sexually reckless / sleazy / whore-coded
* Darkly funny
* Argumentative
* Curious about other dissenters
* Open to different worldviews if they share hatred of control
* Unfiltered
* Morally messy
* More entertaining than respectable

## Important nuance

Drift should **not** be framed as a clean ideological leader.

He should be framed as:

> “the wrong guy saying the things polite people won’t say.”

That keeps the brand comedic and chaotic instead of making it feel like propaganda.

---

# Brand personality

## Primary personality traits

**Raw**
No polish. No PR voice. No sanitized summaries.

**Chaotic**
Randomness is not a bug. It is the format.

**Irreverent**
Nothing is sacred. Government, culture, sex, media, and social rules are all fair targets.

**Anti-control**
The enemy is control, censorship, bureaucracy, forced consensus, and people who want to manage speech.

**Darkly funny**
The brand can go ugly, but it should usually keep a comedy fuse burning.

**Open-mic anarchic**
Anyone can say something. The site should feel like the door is kicked open.

---

# Brand enemy

A good brand needs an enemy. DirtyFM’s enemy is not one political party.

The enemy is:

**control.**

More specifically:

* Government overreach
* Censorship
* Corporate-safe speech
* Fake politeness
* Algorithmic sanitization
* Hall-monitor culture
* Bureaucratic language
* Respectability politics
* People who want every conversation pre-approved

The brand should not feel like “left vs right.”
It should feel like:

> “You don’t get to tell me what I’m allowed to say.”

# Brand voice

## Voice rules

DirtyFM should sound like someone talking into a busted mic at 2:00 AM.

Use:

* Short punches
* Profanity
* Imperfect grammar when it adds character
* Mock-serious headlines
* Anti-authority framing
* Comedy-first aggression
* Weird specificity
* Hard opinions
* Abrupt transitions

Avoid:

* Corporate podcast language
* “Join us on a journey”
* Wellness language
* Overexplaining the joke
* Clean influencer tone
* Too much political essay structure
* Trying to sound respectable

## Sample voice

**Normal website copy:**

> DirtyFM is raw internet radio, prank-call chaos, dark comedy, and open-mic ranting for people who still think speech should be dangerous.

**More in-character:**

> This is DirtyFM. Random fucking bullshit, bad ideas, loud mouths, prank calls, government hate, and whatever else Drift drags through the door.

**Episode intro copy:**

> Drift fires up the mic, insults the machinery, lets the freaks speak, and somehow the whole thing turns into a show.

**Blog intro copy:**

> Notes from the dirty signal. Rants, clips, arguments, updates, and whatever crawled out of the broadcast.

# Content pillars

DirtyFM needs buckets so the randomness has structure.

## 1. The Dirty Signal

Main show content.
Rants, monologues, radio clips, raw thoughts.

## 2. Prank Archives

Prank calls, weird phone interactions, hostile comedy, uncomfortable absurdity.

Keep this legally safer by framing the archive as comedy and avoiding instructions for harassment.

## 3. Fuck the Machine

Anti-government, anti-control, censorship, bureaucracy, authority, speech policing.

This should be broad enough to include different viewpoints without pinning the brand to one ideology.

## 4. Open Mic Degeneracy

Audience-submitted rants, calls, voicemails, comments, weird opinions.

## 5. Dirty News

News commentary through Drift’s lens.

Not objective journalism.
More like:

> “Here’s today’s bullshit, interpreted by a man who should not be in charge of anything.”

## 6. Video Trash

YouTube videos, short clips, highlight reels, best-of moments.

Make it feel:

* Legible

## Do not

Make it feel:

* Corporate
* Podcast-bro
* Too patriotic
* Too militia-coded
* Too clean
* Too conspiracy-board cliché
* Too pornographic
* Too hateful
* Like a serious political organization

That last one matters. The brand works better as **comedy chaos with anti-control politics**, not a recruitment page for extremism.

# Tone boundaries

Because the brand is intentionally raw, it needs boundaries so it stays usable as a public website.

## Strong but safer framing

Use:

```text
anti-control
anti-censorship
anti-government overreach
free speech
uncensored comedy
dark humor
prank culture
raw talk
```

Be careful with:

```text
threats
targeted harassment
instructions for illegal acts
calls for violence
hate toward protected groups
```

DirtyFM can be offensive without becoming legally or platform-dangerous.

The site should sell the feeling of danger, not create actual liability.
